# The STaaS Manifesto

## Don't start from scratch. Start from what humanity has already learned.

A founder should not have to discover every relevant dataset, research paper, company precedent, market constraint and validation requirement entirely through trial and error.

The knowledge needed to build a company already exists in fragments: academic research, public datasets, company histories, product launches, failures, pivots, regulation, standards, market reports, practitioner experience and the lessons of other founders. Yet finding it, judging its relevance and translating it into a decision can be difficult, expensive and time-consuming.

**STaaS (Strategy as a Service) exists to reduce the distance between accumulated knowledge and informed action.**

It is a founder-centred approach to strategic learning and decision support: bringing relevant knowledge together, helping people reason with it, and making the path from uncertainty to a testable decision clearer.

## Learning from visual explanations and accumulated knowledge

The educational approach of 3Blue1Brown offers a useful parallel: make difficult ideas understandable by revealing their structure, then help learners build intuition rather than merely memorise answers. STaaS applies that principle to strategy by making the links between evidence, context, assumptions and decisions inspectable. The comparison is methodological; no endorsement or affiliation is implied. See the [3Blue1Brown learning path and STaaS analysis](docs/3BLUE1BROWN-AND-STAAS.md).

## The problem is not a lack of information

Founders are surrounded by information but often lack the time, context, expertise or connections to answer the questions that matter:

- What has already been tried, by whom, and with what outcome?
- Which research, datasets and precedents are relevant to this problem?
- What differs across this market, geography, customer group or regulatory environment?
- Which assumptions are supported, contradicted or still untested?
- What evidence would make this decision safer or more credible?
- What should be learned before investing more time, money or effort?

A search result is not an answer. A business plan is not validation. A confident AI response is not evidence. A framework applied without context is not strategy. The work is to connect information to the decision it can legitimately support.

## Our principles

### 1. Start with the problem, not the fashionable solution
Understand the people, constraints and conditions involved before prescribing a product, technology or business model. Revisit the original problem definition when new evidence changes it.

### 2. Make accumulated knowledge discoverable
Help founders find relevant research, datasets, company precedents, market intelligence, standards, regulation, funding pathways and specialist expertise. Make clear what is accessible, restricted or unknown.

### 3. Ground analysis in traceable evidence
Record where claims came from, who published them, when they were produced, what populations and contexts they cover, and what limitations apply. Distinguish source facts from interpretation, inference and recommendation.

### 4. Treat context as essential
A strategy that worked for one company, country, customer group or economic environment may not transfer unchanged. Surface local realities, institutions, infrastructure, culture, purchasing power and regulatory differences rather than assuming one market is the default.

### 5. Use AI to augment judgement, not replace it
Models can diagnose, explain, compare, challenge, synthesise and write. Fluency does not guarantee truth, completeness or sound judgement. Evaluate model reasoning separately from evidence retrieval and workflow performance. Make uncertainty visible.

### 6. Turn recommendations into validation
Identify what to test, why it matters, what success or failure looks like, and what decision follows. In regulated or safety-critical domains, identify qualified experts, permissions, ethics processes and regulatory pathways. AI recommendations are not certification or approval.

### 7. Preserve learning beyond a conversation
Decisions, sources, assumptions, experiments and outcomes should form a reusable learning record. Founders should not need to reconstruct their context every time they change model, tool, adviser or platform.

### 8. Build capability, not dependency
The goal is not to make founders reliant on STaaS. It is to help them become better-informed, more independent and more capable of questioning assumptions and executing decisions.

## The story behind the principle

The journey of Ghanaian AI entrepreneur Darlington Akogo and minoHealth AI Labs illustrates why this matters. His publicly documented account describes moving from an initial observation about healthcare access towards a deeper understanding of diagnostic capacity, consulting healthcare professionals, studying research, exploring technical approaches, collecting data and confronting the credibility, validation and regulatory demands of healthcare AI.

His international advocacy has highlighted the strategic importance of building close to the people and conditions a solution is intended to serve. The lesson is not that every founder should follow the same path. It is that meaningful innovation requires problem understanding, domain knowledge, appropriate data, local context and validation, not merely a promising model or appealing pitch.

See the [minoHealth case study and source notes in the project README](README.md#case-study-darlington-akogo-minohealth-ai-labs-and-the-work-behind-locally-grounded-ai).

## What STaaS aims to do

STaaS is intended to help founders move through a continuous cycle:

1. **Discover** relevant knowledge, evidence, precedents and constraints.
2. **Qualify** sources for credibility, date, relevance, access rights and limitations.
3. **Understand** the problem, customer, context and assumptions.
4. **Analyse** options using appropriate strategic frameworks and transparent reasoning.
5. **Challenge** conclusions, identify gaps and consider alternatives.
6. **Validate** uncertain claims through research, expert engagement and real-world experiments.
7. **Act** through measurable priorities, milestones and decisions.
8. **Learn** from outcomes and preserve evidence and reasoning for future decisions.

This is an ambition and product direction, not a claim that every capability is already implemented. The repository distinguishes the current prototype from proposed capabilities.

## A platform, not a single model

No single language model contains all current, relevant and verified knowledge a founder may need. No retrieval system guarantees that every important source has been found. No framework decides what matters without context.

STaaS treats useful strategic intelligence as a system:
- models provide reasoning and synthesis;
- research and evidence sources provide material to examine;
- retrieval and provenance connect claims to sources;
- structured parameters and frameworks make analysis explicit;
- deterministic tools check calculations, schemas and workflow constraints;
- relevant experts review consequential questions;
- validation and feedback improve the founder's understanding.

The model is a reasoning component, not the entire intelligence system. Assess the platform on whether it helps people make better-grounded decisions, not simply on how convincing its prose sounds.

## What we will not claim

STaaS will not guarantee business success, replace experienced advisers, certify regulated products, grant access to restricted datasets or independently verify every model-generated statement. It will not treat correlation as causation, an anecdote as universal proof, a benchmark score as real-world impact or a generated recommendation as a validated decision.

Where evidence is missing, say so. Where sources conflict, show the disagreement. Where specialist judgement is required, identify that boundary.

## The promise

A founder should spend less time rediscovering what is already known and more time understanding what is unique about their problem, testing what remains uncertain and building something that works.

**Don't start from scratch. Start from what humanity has already learned, and build what comes next.**
