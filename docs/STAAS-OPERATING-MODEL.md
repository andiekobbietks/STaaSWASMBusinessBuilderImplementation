# STaaS operating model

## Definition

**Strategy as a Service (STaaS)** is a recurring strategy-to-execution operating model. It provides an organisation with a structured way to decide what matters, translate decisions into owned work, measure outcomes and adapt as evidence changes.

It is not merely outsourced advice. It is not the same thing as SaaS. Software can support the service, but the value comes from continuity, context, accountability and a disciplined execution loop.

## The operating cycle

| Step | Activity | Tangible artefact |
|---|---|---|
| Discover | Understand goals, customers, resources and constraints | Business context brief |
| Diagnose | Identify gaps, risks, assumptions and opportunities | Diagnosis and risk register |
| Strategise | Set direction, positioning and choices | Strategy brief and decision record |
| Prioritise | Select the next highest-value work | Prioritised backlog |
| Execute | Deliver approved tasks and experiments | Milestones and delivery updates |
| Measure | Compare outcomes with objectives | KPI and learning review |
| Adapt | Change priorities in response to evidence | Updated strategy and backlog |

## Accountability and customer control

The customer should retain ownership and control of:
- business decisions and strategy approval;
- accounts, domains, data, source code and digital assets;
- third-party subscriptions and vendor relationships;
- budgets, payments and spending authorisation;
- access permissions and the right to revoke them;
- acceptance of deliverables and the decision to continue or exit.

A provider can be accountable for:
- agreed discovery and analysis;
- documenting options, assumptions and recommendations;
- maintaining the agreed backlog and delivery cadence;
- completing explicitly scoped work;
- reporting status, blockers, costs and risks;
- keeping records of decisions and approvals;
- documenting handover and exit.

A provider should never imply it can guarantee the customer's commercial outcomes.

## Service components

A possible managed engagement can combine:
1. Initial discovery and baseline.
2. A rolling strategy and priorities document.
3. A jointly approved backlog.
4. A regular delivery and review cadence.
5. Measurement against agreed indicators.
6. Transparent reporting of work, risks and expenditure.
7. Optional implementation work, separately scoped where appropriate.
8. A documented handover and exit path.

## Conversational delivery and integrations

The software may provide a chat-based interface for discovery, analysis, research and execution planning. A candidate implementation uses the Vercel AI SDK for streaming, model-provider abstraction, tool orchestration and React chat UI. MCP may expose approved capabilities to compatible external clients; MCP Apps may provide interactive interfaces for selected tools in compatible hosts.

This is a delivery interface, not a transfer of accountability to the model. The customer remains in control of business decisions, connected accounts, external actions, budgets and approvals. Every integration must have a defined purpose, data scope, owner, access method, failure behaviour and revocation route.

A managed STaaS engagement may use the chat workspace to capture requests, prepare recommendations, show evidence, coordinate approved work and report progress. Human delivery partners remain accountable for the agreed service work; AI-generated suggestions are not automatically approved instructions.

## Commercial hypotheses

Possible commercial structures include:
- a fixed-scope discovery engagement;
- a recurring strategy and review retainer;
- an adviser-supported package;
- separately priced implementation work;
- specialist partner work with explicit customer approval.

Do not publish prices until discovery validates willingness to pay, delivery effort, support expectations and gross margin. Separate provider fees from software subscriptions and third-party costs.

## Evidence and trust

Every meaningful recommendation should state:
- what is known;
- what is assumed;
- what evidence supports it;
- what remains uncertain;
- why the recommendation is preferred;
- what action would test it;
- who owns the next step;
- when it should be reviewed.

The operating model succeeds when it improves decision quality and execution discipline, not when it produces more documents.

## Relationship to this application

The current application is an early idea analyzer. It provides a first structured pass through Clarity, Niche, Action and Strategy. It does not yet implement the full operating cycle above. The business profile, evidence register, assumption ledger, decision log, roadmap, metrics, review cadence and managed-service workflow remain proposed capabilities.
