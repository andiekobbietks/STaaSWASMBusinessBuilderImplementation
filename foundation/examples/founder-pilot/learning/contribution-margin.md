# Learning objective

Explain how much revenue remains after variable costs for each unit sold, and why revenue alone does not show whether a product is economically viable.

## Orient

Contribution margin helps a founder understand how much each sale contributes toward fixed costs and profit after variable costs.

## Model: worked example

In this fictional example:
- selling price per lunch: £8.00;
- variable cost per lunch: £3.20;
- contribution per lunch: £4.80;
- contribution margin: 60 percent.

The [attested computation](../metrics/contribution-margin.md) shows the deterministic calculation.

## Contrast: counterexample

A business can generate £10,000 in revenue and still have weak contribution if ingredients, packaging, payment fees and delivery costs consume most of the sales value.

Revenue growth alone is not proof of improving unit economics.

## Retrieve

Without looking at the worked example, explain the difference between revenue, contribution and contribution margin.

## Apply

Estimate the contribution margin for the founder's own offer. Identify which costs change with each additional sale and which costs are fixed.

## Reflect

What cost did you initially overlook? What evidence would change your estimate?

## Revisit

Repeat the retrieval and application exercise after the pilot has produced real cost and payment data.
